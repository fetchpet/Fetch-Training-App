What this is

Fetch as a feed. It opens, it plays, and it keeps playing. There is no menu, no card, no dashboard and nothing to read except the line on the video itself.

The video is the instruction

The clip that autoplays is not an archive of your dog. It is a demo of what you do at this exact step — the hand, the word, the timing — beat by beat. So "how to train this" stopped being a thing you tap into and became the video.

Every script ends on the common mistake, in red: saying the word at the same time as the hand, keeping food in your fist, pushing a frightened dog to the next number. That's the bit other apps leave out.

Three swipes

Right = got it. The step ticks, and the next one starts playing on its own.
Left = didn't get it. Back a step, and that step stops being ticked.
Down = something new. A different behaviour.

The one button is record: film yourself, or him, doing the thing you just watched.

Filming, and what happens to the film

Press record, count down from three, and it films until you press it again — up to a minute. You decide how long a rep takes: a sit is four seconds, a settle is forty.

Every film is kept on the device, in IndexedDB, with the date and the exact step it was filmed at. It survives a refresh. Nothing is uploaded, and there is no account.

The timeline

The 🎞 button opens every film you've taken, per behaviour, newest first. Each one carries the step he was on and whether that was up, the same, or back down from the film before it.

At the top, once there are two or more: then and now, side by side. Same dog, same behaviour, months apart. That comparison is the whole product — it's the thing memory can't do for you.

Going backwards is shown, not hidden: "He has come back down 1 step. That happens to every dog." Deleting a film takes two taps and says out loud that it isn't backed up anywhere.

About the picture quality

There is no real footage in here, and there never was. Every scene is drawn — a flat illustration of the room, with the dog, the hand and the treat animated on top of it. It stands in for the clip a real user would have filmed.

Three ways to put actual video in:

1. Drag a video file onto the phone. It becomes the step you're looking at, straight away.
2. Put files in a clips/ folder next to this file, named sit-4.mp4, recall-1.mp4 and so on — behaviour, then rung number. They load on open.
3. Press the record button and film. Only works over localhost, not from a file:// path — browsers won't hand over a camera otherwise.

Why video is the mechanism

The whole problem was that progress is real but unfeelable — memory habituates. A row of clips left-to-right defeats that without a single chart. Rung 1 next to rung 4 is the evidence.

Parked, not deleted: the ladder sheet, the then-and-now share, and the full-screen how-to player. They're still in the file and can come back if the feed turns out to be too thin.

Still true

No streaks. Nothing is ever "mastered" — rung 6 is keeping it. Slips are shown, named and explained rather than hidden. Sharing is the OS share sheet, nobody else's feed.
